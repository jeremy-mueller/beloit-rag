# Test Questions
## Direct
1. What is the minimum number of units a student must be registered for to be considered full-time?

Expected answer: At least 3.0 units. Three-quarter time is at least 2.25 units and
half time is at least 1.50 units. The normal course load is 4–4.25 units.

Source: `catalog-adding-dropping-withdrawing` (catalog p13)

2. Which students are exempt from the requirement to live in College housing during their first six credit terms?

Expected answer: Married students, military veterans with two years of active duty,
students with dependent children, and commuting students who live with their
parents or legal guardians within a 30-minute commute. Any other exception needs prior
approval of the Director of Residence Life.

Source: `handbook-housing-living-on-campus` (handbook pp. 66–67)

3. Who must approve an emotional support animal before a student brings it to campus?

Expected answer: The Director of Learning Enrichment and Disability Services.
Students must receive ESA designation from the Director *before* bringing the animal to
campus; without that authorization an animal cannot be kept on campus and must be removed
immediately if found. Approval is case-by-case and requires a documented disability, that
the animal is necessary for equal use and enjoyment of the dwelling, and a documented
nexus between the two.

Source: `handbook-disability-and-support-animals` (handbook p58)

4. How many hours per week may an hourly student worker work while classes are in session?

Expected answer: Up to 20 hours per week during the academic year while classes are
in session.

Source: `union-student-workers-agreement` (Article 15: Hours Worked)

## Two Documents
5. A student misses two continuous weeks of a course and is administratively dropped. What happens to their course record, and what happens to their financial aid?

Expected answer: Two separate consequences from two separate documents.

Course record: Once a student misses two continuous weeks, the instructor must submit
an alert slip to the Associate Dean of Students, who may begin the administrative drop
process in consultation with the Registrar. A student who discontinues attendance without
officially withdrawing may receive an F. A student administratively dropped may be
administratively withdrawn from the semester unless the Associate Dean of Students and
the Registrar permit part-time status.

Financial aid: Separately, satisfactory academic progress requires completing and
earning credit for 67% or more of the units enrolled in. A dropped course still counts as
enrolled, so falling under 67% triggers a warning notice from the Financial Aid Office,
one additional semester of eligibility, and then suspension of aid if the standard still
is not met.

Sources: `handbook-class-attendance` (handbook pp. 17–19) and
`catalog-satisfactory-academic-progress` (catalog pp. 216–217)

6. A transfer student arrives with 45 semester hours from a community college and three AP exams scored 4 or 5. How much of that counts toward a Beloit degree, and are they required to live on campus?

Expected answer:

Credit: No more than 60 semester hours (90 quarter hours) of transfer work may be
applied toward the Beloit degree, and only liberal arts courses with a minimum grade of C
are credited — so the 45 hours are under the cap but only the qualifying courses count.
Separately, up to 8 units total may be applied from tests administered outside the
college; AP subject scores of 4 and 5 generally earn 1 unit each, so the three exams are
worth up to 3 units.

Housing: Transfer students under 21 are required to live in the residence halls and eat
in the dining hall, per the policies for the class or term the Registrar assigns them.

Sources: `catalog-admission-and-transfer-credit` (catalog pp. 208–210) **and**
`handbook-housing-living-on-campus` (handbook pp. 66–67)

7. A student withdraws from the college during the third week of the semester. How much tuition is refunded, and what happens to their institutional scholarship?

Expected answer:

Tuition: 60%. The refund schedule for fall and spring gives 100% for days 1–5 (week 1),
80% for days 6–12 (week 2), 60% for days 13–19 (week 3), 40% for week 4, 20% for week 5,
and nothing after the last day of the fifth week. A week runs Sunday to Saturday and a
short first week counts as a full week. No refunds of any kind are made if the student was
dismissed or suspended.

Institutional aid: Institutional aid is adjusted to match the Student Accounts refund
policy — the student retains the same percentage of institutional scholarship, grant, or
loan as the percentage their tuition charge was reduced to. Federal and state aid is
handled separately under Return of Title IV Funds, earned pro rata by the portion of the
semester completed.

Sources: `catalog-tuition-payment-and-refunds` (catalog pp. 211–212) and
`catalog-financial-aid-and-r2t4` (catalog pp. 213–215)

## Student Phrased
8. Can I still drop this class without it showing up on my transcript?

Expected answer: Yes, if dropped before the beginning of the second half of the
course — a course dropped before that point is expunged from the record. Dropped after
that, a grade of F is recorded unless the Academic Performance Committee approves a late
drop or a grade of WP on petition. The student must submit a drop card signed by the
instructor and advisor before the deadline; turning it in late means a $25 fine per
course. A late-drop petition is only considered within one calendar year of the end of
that semester.

Source: `catalog-adding-dropping-withdrawing` (catalog p14)

9. Can I keep my cat in my room if it's an emotional support animal?

Expected answer: Only with prior approval. An ESA may be a reasonable accommodation
within the student's privately assigned living space, but designation must come from the
Director of Learning Enrichment and Disability Services *before* the animal comes to
campus, following the deadlines in the Housing and Food Service Accommodation policy. An
animal found on campus without approval must be removed immediately. Approval requires a
documented disability, that the animal is necessary for equal opportunity to use and enjoy
the dwelling, and a documented nexus between the disability and the animal's assistance.
Online services that "register" or "certify" an animal for a fee are not valid.

Source: `handbook-disability-and-support-animals` (handbook pp. 57–60)
