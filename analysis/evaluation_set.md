### Q1 — direct

**Question:** What is the minimum number of units a student must be registered for to be considered full-time?

**Gold answer:** At least 3.0 units. Three-quarter time is at least 2.25 units and half time is at least 1.50 units. The normal course load is 4–4.25 units.

### Q2 — direct

**Question:** Which students are exempt from the requirement to live in College housing during their first six credit terms?

**Gold answer:** Married students, military veterans with two years of active duty, students with dependent children, and commuting students who live with their parents or legal guardians within a 30-minute commute. Any other exception needs prior approval of the Director of Residence Life.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `handbook-housing-living-on-campus` (handbook pp66–67)

### Q3 — direct

**Question:** Who must approve an emotional support animal before a student brings it to campus?

**Gold answer:** The Director of Learning Enrichment and Disability Services. Students must receive ESA designation from the Director *before* bringing the animal to campus; without that authorization an animal cannot be kept on campus and must be removed immediately if found. Approval is case-by-case and requires a documented disability, that the animal is necessary for equal use and enjoyment of the dwelling, and a documented nexus between the two.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `handbook-disability-and-support-animals` (handbook p58)

### Q5 — multi-doc

**Question:** A student misses two continuous weeks of a course and is administratively dropped. What happens to their course record, and what happens to their financial aid?

**Gold answer:** Two separate consequences from two separate documents.

*Course record:* Once a student misses two continuous weeks, the instructor must submit an alert slip to the Associate Dean of Students, who may begin the administrative drop process in consultation with the Registrar. A student who discontinues attendance without officially withdrawing may receive an F. A student administratively dropped may be administratively withdrawn from the semester unless the Associate Dean of Students and the Registrar permit part-time status.

*Financial aid:* Satisfactory academic progress requires completing and earning credit for 67% or more of the units enrolled in. A dropped course still counts as enrolled, so falling under 67% triggers a warning notice from the Financial Aid Office, one additional semester of eligibility, and then suspension of aid if the standard still is not met.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `handbook-class-attendance` (handbook pp17–19) **and** `catalog-satisfactory-academic-progress` (catalog pp216–217)

### Q6 — multi-doc

**Question:** A transfer student arrives with 45 semester hours from a community college and three AP exams scored 4 or 5. How much of that counts toward a Beloit degree, and are they required to live on campus?

**Gold answer:**

*Credit:* No more than 60 semester hours (90 quarter hours) of transfer work may be applied toward the Beloit degree, and only liberal arts courses with a minimum grade of C are credited — so the 45 hours are under the cap but only the qualifying courses count. Separately, up to 8 units total may be applied from tests administered outside the college; AP subject scores of 4 and 5 generally earn 1 unit each, so the three exams are worth up to 3 units.

*Housing:* Transfer students under 21 are required to live in the residence halls and eat in the dining hall, per the policies for the class or term the Registrar assigns them.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `catalog-admission-and-transfer-credit` (catalog pp208–210) **and** `handbook-housing-living-on-campus` (handbook pp66–67)

### Q7 — multi-doc

**Question:** A student withdraws from the college during the third week of the semester. How much tuition is refunded, and what happens to their institutional scholarship?

**Gold answer:**

*Tuition:* 60%. The refund schedule for fall and spring gives 100% for days 1–5 (week 1), 80% for days 6–12 (week 2), 60% for days 13–19 (week 3), 40% for week 4, 20% for week 5, and nothing after the last day of the fifth week. A week runs Sunday to Saturday and a short first week counts as a full week. No refunds of any kind are made if the student was dismissed or suspended.

*Institutional aid:* Institutional aid is adjusted to match the Student Accounts refund policy — the student retains the same percentage of institutional scholarship, grant, or loan as the percentage their tuition charge was reduced to. Federal and state aid is handled separately under Return of Title IV Funds, earned pro rata by the portion of the semester completed.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `catalog-tuition-payment-and-refunds` (catalog pp211–212) **and** `catalog-financial-aid-and-r2t4` (catalog pp213–215)

### Q8 — student-phrased

**Question:** "Can I still drop this class without it showing up on my transcript?"

**Gold answer:** Yes, if dropped before the beginning of the second half of the course — a course dropped before that point is expunged from the record. Dropped after that, a grade of F is recorded unless the Academic Performance Committee approves a late drop or a grade of WP on petition. The student must submit a drop card signed by the instructor and advisor before the deadline; turning it in late means a $25 fine per course. A late-drop petition is only considered within one calendar year of the end of that semester.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `catalog-adding-dropping-withdrawing` (catalog p14)

### Q9 — student-phrased

**Question:** "Can I keep my cat in my room if it's an emotional support animal?"

**Gold answer:** Only with prior approval. An ESA may be a reasonable accommodation within the student's privately assigned living space, but designation must come from the Director of Learning Enrichment and Disability Services *before* the animal comes to campus, following the deadlines in the Housing and Food Service Accommodation policy. An animal found on campus without approval must be removed immediately. Approval requires a documented disability, that the animal is necessary for equal opportunity to use and enjoy the dwelling, and a documented nexus between the disability and the animal's assistance. Online services that "register" or "certify" an animal for a fee are not valid.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `handbook-disability-and-support-animals` (handbook pp57–60)

### Q14 — direct

**Question:** How long does a student have to finish an incomplete, and what happens if they don't?

**Gold answer:** Unless the instructor stipulates a shorter period, an incomplete must be satisfied within eight weeks of the last final exam day of the semester. Incompletes normally convert to a grade of F at the end of the eight-week period unless a request for an extension has been approved. In exceptional cases such as lengthy illness the instructor may petition the Academic Performance Committee or its designee to extend the period. While an "I" remains on the record the student may not enroll for credit in any course that has that course as a stated prerequisite, and may not graduate.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `catalog-grading-and-incompletes` (catalog p17)

### Q20 — multi-doc

**Question:** A student has 24 earned units and wants to take a course at another college next term. What class year are they, and how much credit can they transfer?

**Gold answer:**

*Classification:* Senior. Classification by earned units is: first-year fewer than 7, sophomore 7 to 14.999, junior 15 to 22.999, senior 23 or more.

*Transfer:* No more than 2 units of credit may be transferred during the senior year, except with the approval of the Academic Performance Committee. The student also needs advance permission of the advisor and the registrar, should complete a Transfer Credit Authorization Form before enrolling elsewhere, and only courses with a grade of C or better are eligible. Grades from transfer courses are not recorded on the transcript and are not included in the Beloit GPA.

### Q21 — multi-doc

**Question:** A student earns a 3.5 GPA this semester but has an incomplete in one course. Can they make the dean's list?

**Gold answer:** No. Dean's list requires at least a 3.400 cumulative grade point average for the semester, at least 3 graded units, and no grade of "F" or "I" in any course that semester. Students on academic probation or with incompletes from previous terms also cannot earn dean's list recognition. The 3.5 clears the GPA threshold, but the incomplete disqualifies them. Separately, the incomplete must be satisfied within eight weeks of the last final exam day or it normally converts to an F.

### Q22 — multi-doc

**Question:** A first-year student arrives with AP Statistics scored 5 and AP Psychology scored 4. What credit do they get, and does that satisfy the quantitative reasoning requirement?

**Gold answer:**

*Credit:* AP subject examination scores of 4 and 5 guarantee 1 unit of credit and placement, so 2 units total. AP Statistics earns credit for MATH 106 and AP Psychology earns credit for PSYC 100. Up to 8 units of credit from tests administered outside the College may be applied toward graduation.

*Requirement:* No. AP credits may not be used to fulfill any of the Domain or Skills requirements, and the catalog states that AP/IB/GCE A-level credits may not be used to meet domain, skill, experience, or capstone requirements. The student must still complete a designated Q course. (A transfer course from another college *may* fulfill the Q requirement — the exclusion is specific to exam credit.)

**Evidence doc id(s):** `registrar-transfer-ap-gce-ib-credit` **and** `catalog-domain-and-unit-requirements` (catalog p11)

### Q23 — multi-doc

**Question:** A student fails a course in their first semester and ends with a 0.9 cumulative GPA. Can they repeat the course, and what is their academic standing?

**Gold answer:**

*Repeating:* Yes. Students may repeat a course if they received a grade of D+ or lower. The grade from the most recent attempt is used in calculating the GPA and earned unit count; credit for the previous attempt is set to zero and the previous grade is not used in determining the GPA. The letter grade appears on the transcript with an "R" prefix. Faculty of the repeated course determine if and when a student may repeat it, and space is not guaranteed.

*Standing:* A cumulative GPA below 1.0 after the first term makes a student subject to academic dismissal. However, normally first-term first-year students are not dismissed for academic deficiency but are instead warned, placed on academic probation, or suspended — though the Academic Performance Committee may dismiss a first-year student if its findings indicate that action is appropriate in the individual case.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `catalog-grading-and-incompletes` (catalog p16) **and** `catalog-academic-standing` (catalog p15)

### Q24 — multi-doc

**Question:** Can a transfer student use courses from their previous college to satisfy Beloit's writing, quantitative reasoning, and intercultural literacy requirements?

**Gold answer:** Partly, and the three differ.

- **Writing:** only 1 of the 3 required writing courses may be a transferred course.
- **Quantitative reasoning:** the Q requirement may be fulfilled with a transfer course.
- **Intercultural literacy:** must be fulfilled at Beloit College.

AP/IB/GCE A-level credits may not be used for any of these. Each of the W, Q, and C requirements must be satisfied by a different course. Students who have earned an Associate of Arts or Associate of Science degree automatically fulfill the domain and skill requirements.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `catalog-writing-quantitative-intercultural` (catalog pp7–9) **and** `registrar-transfer-ap-gce-ib-credit`

### Q25 — student-phrased

**Question:** "I bombed a class last year. If I retake it, does the old grade still count against my GPA?"

**Gold answer:** No. If the original grade was D+ or lower the course may be repeated, and only the most recent attempt counts toward the grade point average and earned unit count; credit for the previous attempt is set to zero and the previous grade is not used in determining the GPA. The grade appears on the transcript with an "R" prefix. The policy applies only to courses taken at Beloit College, applies to topics courses only if the same topic is repeated, and does not apply to music lessons and ensembles or to dance courses that may be taken twice for credit.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `catalog-grading-and-incompletes` (catalog p16)

### Q26 — multi-doc (student-phrased)

**Question:** "Can my parents see my grades?"

**Gold answer:** Not automatically.

*Grades:* Under FERPA the college maintains the privacy of academic records. Grade reports are available to students through the Portal at the end of each semester. A student may request in writing that final grades be sent to their parent(s).

*Directory information:* Separately, college "directory information" may be released publicly unless the student restricts it by written notice to the registrar by the end of the first week of classes during any given semester. Directory information includes name, address, telephone listing, date of birth, academic major, participation in officially recognized sports and activities, weight and height of members of athletic teams, dates of attendance, degrees and awards received, most recent previous school attended, and enrollment status. Grades are not directory information.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `catalog-grading-and-incompletes` (catalog p17) **and** `catalog-student-records-and-honors` (catalog p18)

### Q27 — student-phrased

**Question:** "I took an online class over the summer at my community college. Will it count?"

**Gold answer:** It can, within limits. Courses with a grade of "C" or better from accredited colleges are eligible, and work is recorded in equivalent units where one unit equals 4 semester hours or 6 quarter hours. No more than 8 Beloit units (32 semester hours / 48 quarter hours) of distance learning coursework — online, extension, or correspondence — will be allowed toward the degree; courses taken under Beloit's aegis, whether through Acadeum or Beloit itself, do not count toward that 8-unit limit. Students enrolled at Beloit should complete a Transfer Credit Authorization Form before taking a course elsewhere, and official transcripts should be presented to the registrar as soon as possible. Grades from transfer courses are not recorded on the transcript and are not included in the Beloit GPA.

**Evidence doc id(s):** `registrar-transfer-ap-gce-ib-credit`
