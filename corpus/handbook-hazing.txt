4. Hazing Beloit College believes that establishing a strong learning community is critical to fully experiencing a liberal arts education. We highly encourage students to be involved in co-curricular activities, which can enhance both social and academic growth. Traditions, rituals and rites of passage help to develop a sense of community at Beloit and we value these organizational bonding opportunities. Groups who have their members participate in these activities must ensure that they are positive experiences for all involved. A. Stop Campus Hazing Act The Stop Campus Hazing Act (Hazing Act) requires institutions of higher education (IHEs) that participate in federal student aid programs to report hazing incidents. It also renames the Jeanne Clery Disclosure of Campus Security Policy and Campus Crime Statistics Act as the Jeanne Clery Campus Safety Act. Speciﬁcally, the act requires each IHE to disclose hazing incidents that were reported to campus security authorities or local police agencies in its annual security report. It also requires the College to enact prevention measures. The Act deﬁnes the term hazing to mean any intentional, knowing, or reckless act committed by a person (whether individually or in concert with other persons) against another person or persons regardless of the willingness of such other person or persons to participate, that (1) is committed in the course of an initiation into, an aﬃliation with, or the maintenance of membership in, a student organization (e.g., a club, athletic team, fraternity, or sorority); and (2) causes or creates a risk, above the reasonable risk encountered in the course of participation in the IHE or the organization, of physical or psychological injury. B. Campus Hazing Transparency Report The Campus Hazing Transparency Report will be published by the Safety and Security Department at the same time as the Annual Security Report is published. If another is needed, it will be published sometime during the Spring or Summer semesters to the Security website. Notiﬁcation via email to beloit.edu email addresses will be sent when one is published C. Beloit College Hazing Deﬁnition The Beloit College deﬁnition of hazing is consistent with the Stop Campus Hazing Act but is more detailed. In addition to the Hazing Act, Wisconsin state laws prohibit hazing in all forms ( Wisconsin State Statute, 948.51 Hazing ). Acts that cause physical, mental, or emotional harm are against College policy and will not be tolerated. Any group members or organizations (i.e. within sports teams, clubs, Greek organizations, unoﬃcial groups, etc.) suspected of hazing will have their behavior reviewed by College oﬃcials and individuals and/or groups suspected of hazing will follow the normal judicial investigation process.

It is important to note the social pressure can constitute forced or requisite participation, even if the activity is claimed to be voluntary. The following behaviors and activities constitute hazing on this campus: i. Actions that recklessly or intentionally endanger the physical and mental health or safety of students. ii. Forced or required consumption or purchase of any food, liquor, drug, beverage, water, or any other substance. iii. Requiring, coercing, or pressuring any person to participate in an activity, ritual, tradition, or conduct that humiliates, degrades, intimidates, ostracizes, endangers, abuses, or compels unwanted acts or duties, particularly where the activity is unrelated to the legitimate purposes of the organization or group, regardless of the individual's willingness to participate. iv. Forced [and unhealthy] exposure to the weather. v. Excessive fatigue resulting from sleep deprivation, physical activities, or exercise. vi. Assignment of activities that would be illegal or unlawful, or might be morally oﬀensive to a reasonable person. vii. Physical brutality, including paddling, striking (with ﬁsts, open hands, or objects) or branding or requiring a tattoo. viii. Kidnapping and forced transportation or stranding of individuals, and any activities involving forced blindfolds of any sort. ix. Verbal abuse, including “line-ups,” interrogations, and berating of individuals. x. Forced or required conduct that could embarrass or adversely aﬀect the dignity of the individual (and/or the College), including the performance of public stunts, property defacement, activities, and/or buﬀoonery. xi. The intentional creation of clean-up work or labor for new members by active members or alumni. xii. Denial of suﬃcient time and energy to devote to class attendance and/or academic assignments. xiii. Forced or required nudity or lewd behavior. NOTE: It is important to note that an active member participating in any of these activities in addition to the inductees does not mean that the activity is not hazing.

Hazing and Greek Organizations The Inter Fraternal Panhellenic Council (IFPC), consisting of representatives from all campus Greek organizations, may not probe into the internal aﬀairs of any member organization such as new member training programs, initiation rites, and all other activities unique to a member house. If a member or potential member of a Greek organization is suspected of hazing, the members of the IFPC are required to report it to the Dean of Students oﬃce. Wisconsin Hazing Law The Stop Campus Hazing Act deﬁnition i s consistent with the Wisconsin State Statute, 948.51 Hazing . Wisconsin Statute 948.51 deﬁnes Hazing as: (1) In this section “forced activity” means any activity which is a condition of initiation or admission into or aﬃliation with an organization, regardless of a student’s willingness to participate in the activity. (2) No person may intentionally or recklessly engage in acts which endanger the physical health or safety of a student for the purpose of initiation or admission into or aﬃliation with any organization operating in connection with a school, college or university. Under those circumstances, prohibited acts may include any brutality of a physical nature, such as whipping, beating, branding, forced consumption of any food, liquor, drug or other substance, forced conﬁnement or any other forced activity which endangers the physical health or safety of the student. (3) Whoever violates sub. (2) is guilty of: (a) A Class A misdemeanor if the act results in or is likely to result in bodily harm to another. (b) A Class H felony if the act results in great bodily harm to another. (c) A Class G felony if the act results in the death of another. Per Wisconsin Statute 939.50 , penalties, ﬁnes and prison sentences are as follows: i. (a) For a Class A misdemeanor, a ﬁne not to exceed $10,000 or imprisonment not to exceed 9 months, or both. ii. (h) For a Class H felony, a ﬁne not to exceed $10,000 or imprisonment not to exceed 6 years, or both. iii. (g) For a Class G felony, a ﬁne not to exceed $25,000 or imprisonment not to exceed 10 years, or both.
