General Housing Expectations/Policies/Services Beloit College recognizes that learning is not conﬁned to the classroom but also encompasses a broad range of co-curricular activities and experiences. One of the most important of these is the interactions among students, faculty and staﬀ which occur on a residential campus. The residence halls are largely self-governing and operate on the basic principle of mutual respect and consideration for the rights of all residents. Students are encouraged to accept individual and collective responsibilities for setting their own guidelines in these areas. However, the College reserves the right to change room assignments and occupancy levels in the interest of health, discipline, housing needs, or the general welfare of the student and/or other students. In order to ensure smooth functioning of the living areas (residence halls, special interest houses, Greek houses and apartments), the protection of students’ individual rights, and the protection of personal and College property, a number of operating policies and procedures are in eﬀect in the living areas: Air Conditioning: Air conditioning units may not be installed in any residence space. The following residential spaces have A/C (when Facilities turns it on): Aldrich, Moore Town Houses, the Clary Apartments, Peet, Porter, Whitney, Blaisdell, Bushnell, Maurer, Brannon, and Chapin. Alcohol: Please see the full policy ( Section X, A 1 ). Students who are 21 and over may drink alcohol in their rooms, as long as they are following the Alcohol Policy. Bicycles: Bicycle storage space is very limited and only available in Whitney storage on a ﬁrst-come, ﬁrst-serve basis. Students are not to keep bikes in common areas (i.e. attached to stairwells,

[page 69]
furnishings, etc.). Bikes left in these areas will have the locks cut oﬀ and the item removed. Students may leave their bikes outside tethered to the bike racks. It is important to make sure that one’s bike is locked to the rack. Students may not leave their bikes on campus after the academic year unless they will be attending over the summer semester.. Unless speciﬁc arrangements are made with Residence Life, bikes left unattended for more than one month following the end of the academic year are considered abandoned, and may be conﬁscated, sold, donated, or destroyed without compensation. Students are strongly encouraged to have a means to identify a bike as their own and register their bikes with Security. Bulletin Boards: Used as a prime method of communication, each resident is responsible for knowing the contents of all oﬃcial notices on bulletin boards or posting areas. Any graﬃti that is posted or any derogatory or discriminatory comments will be taken down, reported to Security, and the parties involved will be met with by staﬀ and could face potential adjudication. Candles: Candles, kerosene lamps, incense or incense burners (see “Incense” below), or any other open ﬂame devices are not permitted in any of the living areas under any circumstances for ﬁre safety reasons. Check-In/Out: Students may move into the residence halls at the date and time indicated on the website and must be completely checked out of their assigned space by 5 p.m. the day after their last ﬁnal examination , but no later than published dates. Any students seeking an exemption must submit a written request to the Residence Life Oﬃce two weeks prior to the end of the semester. Doors: Suite, hallway, and room doors that are left propped open create a potential security breach and ﬁre hazard. For the sake of all residents, please leave all doors closed and locked (when appropriate). Fire Alarms, Smoke Detectors, and Extinguishers: Fire safety equipment (ﬁre extinguishers, ﬁre alarms, and smoke detectors) and evacuation/exit plans are installed in every building to protect the residents in case of a ﬁre. Tampering with this equipment endangers the safety of others, is a violation of Wisconsin law, and will be considered a serious oﬀense by the College. All tampering and/or damage to ﬁre equipment will result in a ﬁne for the oﬀender, or a ﬁne per person on the ﬂoor where the incident occurred if no speciﬁc person is found to be responsible. Covering a smoke detector in order to render it not operational is a serious violation of policy. In addition, disciplinary action and criminal action may be taken, including ﬁnes and increased room inspections focusing on making sure that the smoke detectors have not been tampered with. Repeated oﬀenses may result in suspension or expulsion. Should the student have a legitimate need to discharge an extinguisher, this action must be reported immediately to the Security Oﬃce; if found that it was a legitimate need, no ﬁnes will be assessed. In all cases, should the individual responsible for the tampering come forward, the ﬁne is likely to be reduced but at the minimum will be responsible for the cost of replacement along with possible disciplinary and

[page 70]
criminal action. In addition to this, there will be two inspections of smoke detectors per year. If a smoke detector is not in place, in addition to ﬁnes, a replacement detector will be installed and the student will be charged the cost of the detector and labor at time and one-half rates. Immediately upon moving into college housing, each resident should become acquainted with the ﬁre exit routes, locations of extinguishers, alarms, and hall evacuation routes and procedures. Fire Evacuation: When a ﬁre alarm sounds, all residents are to evacuate the building according to the following procedures: 1. Notify Security immediately at (608) 363-2355, while moving to exit the building. 2. Evacuation shall be by the nearest exit. Remain calm and exit in an orderly fashion. 3. Students are to move away from the building to a minimum of 300 feet and out of the way of any emergency personnel. Each person in charge of a group (i.e. RA for their ﬂoor) should make a head count to ensure, if possible, that all persons have evacuated the building. All residents of a hall shall meet in the area designated on the hall evacuation plan as the meeting place. If a person is thought to still be in the hall, notify the security oﬃcer and they will immediately notify the ﬁre department. 4. If you know that you caused the ﬁre alarm, approach a security oﬃcer (once you have evacuated) and indicate where/how you think you caused the ﬁre alarm to sound. Students are typically not ﬁned if it appears accidental (i.e. spilled food on a hot burner in the kitchen; set popcorn time too long in the microwave causing it to smoke, etc.), unless the behavior is repeated or appears intentional. 5. DO NOT ENTER or RE-ENTER the building under any circumstances until the all clear has been given. Security will transmit the all-clear information when advised to do so by emergency personnel. Students can be arrested for not evacuating when the ﬁre alarm sounds, tampering with ﬁre extinguishers and smoke detectors, and, obviously, setting ﬁres. Fire Hazards - No Open Flames: Any open ﬂames, including candles, kerosene lamps, incense or incense burners, or any other open ﬂame devices are not permitted in any campus buildings (including residences). Some classrooms may have them under faculty supervision for educational purposes. Fire Pit (Aldrich Field): Use of the Aldrich ﬁrepit is scheduled/approved through Security (608-336-2355 or security@beloit.edu ) - This is the only ﬁrepit allowed to be used on the residential side of campus. Please reach out to the SEAL Director to ensure the supplies are well stocked.

Food Oversight Committee: This is the primary agent for feedback, evaluation, planning, and communication regarding campus dining services. This committee needs student help and input, and, therefore, invites students to share ideas and thoughts during regularly scheduled meetings. To apply for membership with this committee, reach out to the Beloit Student Government or the SEAL Director. Residential Experience Advisory Committee: REAC operates as a point of contact and conversation between campus community members and the residential and student life staﬀ as well as other campus leadership, as needed. Points of conversation may include service oﬀerings, spending priorities, strategic direction, and compliance concerns. REAC represents various community members by communicating with peers and highlighting ideas for improvements. When needed, REAC may acquire relevant data, invite other administrators to participate, and identify resources to consider the feasibility of improvements and assist in prioritizing the timeline for the proposed changes. Furniture Moving: College property, including furnishings, screens/storms, must not be removed from assigned areas without authorization from a professional Residence Life staﬀ member (not peer Resident Assistants). Students who remove furniture or other items from their designated areas will be billed a per day fee and a per item fee for having the item returned to its original location. A student whose room is missing the original furnishings upon check out will be assessed a charge for the replacement value of these items. Halogen Lamps: New halogen lamps with lamp guards may be acceptable, but should be kept away from window curtains. Incense: Incense is not allowed to be burned on campus, including the residence halls. Keys: At the beginning of the semester, at check-in, each student will be issued a key(s)/key card that will open their room and the exterior entrances to other residential buildings during “open” hours. All students must return their keys at the end of each term. Failure to do so will result in a fee for every key not turned in. Keys may not be duplicated. See below (“Lost”) for lost key/card charges. Laundry: Clothes washers and dryers are located in the basement of most residential facilities. Problems with the equipment should be reported directly to the Wash company at 1-424-757-9195, or through their website, wash.com . Please note that all maintenance by the company will occur during Facilities oﬃce hours. There is no cost to the use of the washers and dryers. Lock-Out Policy: Residents are expected to carry room and building keys (and IDs) with them at all times. Locked doors and attention to keys are essential to students’ personal safety and the safety of their property. In the event a student is locked out of their room during a college work day, the student should call the Residence Life Oﬃce (608-363-2350). If it is after oﬃce hours or on the weekend, the student should contact Security, who can let them in. If the student loses their key, they should contact Residence Life immediately, ﬁll out a key replacement form, and take it to Security to have a new key cut for their room.

Lost ID/Meal Cards/Keys/Key Card: Students who have lost their ID card/meal card/key card should report it to Residence Life and ﬁll out a lost ID card form. Residence Life will make you a new card for free if it is your ﬁrst time losing it. Additional cards will have an associated cost to the student. For lost room keys, house keys, and suite keys, there will be a replacement charge (and note that all roommates will need new keys but only the student who lost the key will be charged). If a student wants to dine on campus and does not have their ID/meal card, the student must pay the guest meal price (with credit, food service venues are cashless). However, the student will be issued a receipt and can receive a refund, in full, if the student presents the receipt and their valid ID at the Food Service Oﬃce within 72 hours. ID cards are considered a part of your College record. Falsiﬁcation or tampering with College records (ID/meal card/key card) is considered a serious oﬀense. Violators are subject to disciplinary action, a fee, and possible loss of ID/meal card/key card. Lost keys or IDs are to be taken to the Residence Life Oﬃce (if open) or Security and they will try to track down the owner to return it. Lounges and Public Areas: Lounges are provided for the use of residents and their guests but are not to be used for the accommodation of overnight guests. Students are responsible collectively for the condition of lounges and common areas, such as bathrooms and kitchens, in their part of the building. The costs for repairing any damages to these common areas that cannot be assigned to a speciﬁc individual will be shared by all residents of that area of the building. For safety reasons, residents or their guests may not sleep overnight in the lounge areas. Missing Residents: T he College is obligated by law to endeavor to ﬁnd residential students who are missing. If you must leave campus (even for 24 hours), make sure that your RA, or advisor, or other college oﬃcial knows your whereabouts. See the complete policy . Motorized Vehicles: No motorcycles or other motorized vehicles may be brought into the residence halls. Noise/Quiet Hours/Sound Equipment Policy: The residents of all residence halls have the responsibility to be considerate of others at all times (24 hour courtesy hours). This means respecting another’s right to sleep, study and not be disturbed. Each resident has the responsibility to confront any noise problem that is of concern to them and to call upon a RA if assistance is needed. Each ﬂoor will decide on guidelines to help maintain an atmosphere that will respect the rights of those residents who are studying or sleeping. No stereos or sound equipment should be audible outside one’s room. Residence Life reserves the right to conﬁscate sound ampliﬁcation equipment for repeated or ﬂagrant violations of the noise policy. Again, out of consideration for others we require all residents to observe “Quiet Hours.”

[page 73]
Quiet Hours: ● Sunday-Thursday 10:00 pm-8:00 am ● Friday & Saturday 12:00 am-10:00 am ● During Finals week there are 24-hour quiet hours that are enforced starting on the day before the ﬁrst day of Finals Sound Equipment : Residents are always expected to keep sound equipment at a level conducive to respecting the rights of others in a community. Stereo speakers must be kept out of windows, and not played in a manner disturbing to the college or wider community. This includes Greek houses, special interest houses, and all residential spaces (including outdoor locations such as the Gazebo). All residents are encouraged to use headphones. Students are not to use their rooms for practicing musical instruments, particularly those that may disturb others. Outdoor Areas Use: Use of the housing adjacent outdoor spaces (Aldrich Field, Chapin Quad, and the 609 Quad) for large-scale events, for example: music performances, inﬂatables, or temporary structure, must be approved by the Director of Residential Life. Persons planning such events must also conform to the Program, Activity, and Event Planning and the Social Host policies. Overnight Guests: Residents may have overnight guests for up to three (3) consecutive nights. As host, you need permission from your roommate, and a guest pass from Security. You also need to notify your RA. All guests must comply with College regulations. Hosts are responsible for the conduct and behavior of their guests. (See Guests of Students ) Pets: No pets, other than ﬁsh in an aquarium (5-gallon tank, maximum), are allowed to live on campus with students. Residents having pets that live with them on campus may be required to have the pet removed from campus and/or be required to move oﬀ-campus (and charged the full room rate for the remainder of the semester as a result of not following this regulation). Residents who have pets living with them may also be charged a daily ﬁne, a cleaning charge (to be determined by Facilities ) for each area damaged by the animal, and may be billed for the replacement of ﬂooring in areas damaged by their pet. At times, oﬀ-campus people from the community, or families of students, will bring pets to campus. Those pets are not allowed in any campus building, nor on the grounds of the Stadium area, and waste must be picked up after them in the outdoor venues. Any charges for damages or cleaning are the responsibility of the pet owner, or the student , if the student is found responsible for the presence of the pet. Note: Emotional Support Animals (as veriﬁed by LEADS) and service animals are not considered pets.
