G. Housing Policy - Use & Occupancy of College-Owned Housing 1. Living on Campus Requirements The College’s housing operation, including residence halls, fraternity and sorority housing, and special interest housing, is managed by the Dean of Students (DOS) and Residence Life staﬀ, with the following policies: i. As a residential liberal arts College, we strongly believe that a signiﬁcant amount of personal growth and development occurs in the residential environment. For this reason, full-time students are required to live in College housing and subscribe to a dining plan during their ﬁrst six credit terms. College housing includes residence halls, fraternities, sororities, apartments, special interest houses, and townhouses. Exceptions to this policy include married students, military veterans with two years of active duty, students with dependent children, and commuting students who live with their parents/legal guardians within a 30-minute commute. However, all students are to have current Emergency Contact information submitted via the Portal. Any other exceptions must have prior approval of the Director of Residence Life (DRL).

[page 67]
ii. First-year students must select from one of the top-tier meal plans. Other students may opt into any of the available meal plans. Senior students may choose to opt out of the meal plan. See the Bon Appetit website ( https://beloit-College.cafebonappetit.com/#meal-plans ) for information about the meal plans. For any exceptions, students must have prior approval of the DRL. Commuting students are not required to have the top-tier meal plans, but are encouraged to sign up for one of the other options. iii. Part-time students (under two units of credit) may live in College housing with the approval of the DRL or DOS. iv. Transfer students under 21 years of age are required to live in the residence halls and eat in the dining hall in accordance with policies regulating the class or term to which they are assigned by the Registrar. v. Speciﬁc regulations related to the use and occupancy of College-owned housing will be determined by the DRL. Such regulations are found in the Housing Agreement form and this Handbook. vi. No ﬁrst-year student may live in a special interest house or a fraternity/sorority unless approved by the DRL. vii. If practical, the College will not enter a student’s room unless accompanied by the student, their authorized representative, or a second authorized College representative. However, the College reserves the right to enter student’s rooms for the purpose of inspecting the premises when an authorized agent of the College has reason to believe that: a. an occupant of the room may be physically harmed or endangered; b. damage is being done to the room; c. College policy as stated in the College catalog, Student Handbook, or subsequent written notices is being violated; d. housekeeping, room inspection, maintenance, and/or repair are necessary. In addition, the College oﬃcials conduct room inspections during breaks, to make sure that all the rooms are safe and secure. viii. Items belonging to the College which are not part of furnishings (for example, lounge furniture, dining room equipment, library books) or items which are illegal by law or College policy (e.g., drugs, ﬁrearms) may be removed from a student’s room if they are found via an unobstructed view by a the College oﬃcial. Discovery of such items may constitute the basis for subsequent disciplinary action and/or ﬁnes. ix. Students choosing to room with romantic partners and are involved in a Title IX complaint with their roommate, or exhibit behavior that is objectively disruptive and concerning (e.g., loud arguments, threatening language), will be required to move to alternate housing until an investigation and determination of responsibility is made (or informal resolution is reached). This may occur even if a third party is reporting the incident.
